# Learning Goal 2
**Title: Design and Evaluate Better AI Conversations for Pepper**

## 1. What Will You Achieve?

I will learn how to design, test and improve prompts for the local language model used by Pepper. I will create at least five realistic conversation scenarios for Pepper's intended users and evaluate the model's responses for relevance, clarity, tone, length and usefulness.

I will compare an initial prompt with an improved version and document whether the changes make the responses more suitable for the project.

**I will be finished when:**
I have created at least five tested conversation scenarios, an evaluation checklist, and a documented comparison showing how I improved the AI's responses through prompt changes.

## 2. Why Do You Want To Achieve It?

I want to learn how to guide a language model so that it produces useful responses instead of accepting whatever it generates by default. Connecting an AI model to Pepper is only one part of the project; the quality of the interaction is just as important.

This goal matters to me because prompt design and evaluating AI output are skills I can use in many different AI applications. For the Pepper project, the AI should communicate in a friendly, understandable and respectful way. I want to understand how to make its responses feel natural while keeping them appropriate for the purpose of the robot.

## 3. How Will You Achieve It?

1. Research how system prompts, instructions and conversation history influence language-model responses.
2. Define the role, tone, purpose and boundaries of Pepper's AI assistant.
3. Write at least five conversation scenarios, for example an introduction, talking about hobbies, discussing music, everyday conversation and responding when the model does not know an answer.
4. Test each scenario with the selected Ollama model and save examples of the responses.
5. Create a checklist to assess relevance, clarity, tone, length and usefulness.
6. Improve the system prompt based on the test results and run the same scenarios again.
7. Compare the initial and improved responses, then share the final prompt and findings with the team.

## 4. Who Or What Do You Need?

### People:
- The team member responsible for conversation design and user experience: to agree on Pepper's personality, target users and conversation scenarios.
- The team member responsible for AI integration: to use the final prompts in the Python/Ollama backend.
- My project group and instructor: to review example responses and give feedback.
- Where possible, people familiar with the needs of older adults: to help assess whether the conversations are understandable and appropriate.

### Tools & Software:
- Ollama and the language model selected for Pepper.
- The Python backend, or Ollama's local interface for early prompt experiments.
- A text editor or shared project document for storing prompts.
- A spreadsheet or simple table for recording test results.
- GitHub for versioning the final prompts and documentation.

### Data & Resources:
- Realistic example questions and conversation scenarios.
- Research and guidance about conversational AI and prompt engineering.
- The project's description of its intended users and purpose.
- A response-evaluation checklist.

### Possible Obstacles, And What I Will Do About Them:
- The small model may not follow complex instructions consistently: I will keep prompts clear and concise, test simpler instructions and compare models if needed.
- Responses may be repetitive or unnatural: I will test different prompt structures and conversation examples.
- It may be difficult to judge response quality objectively: I will use the same checklist for each test and ask team members for feedback.
- The model may generate unsuitable or incorrect content: I will include clear boundaries in the prompt and record failures for further testing.
